# Phase 3.6b author overlay

This overlay changes author guidance only. All Phase 3.6a mechanical rules, resolvers, scheduler and atomic commit remain authoritative and unchanged. Freeze this file before production_start.

Before each fresh draft, read that text's actual allowed vocabulary snapshot. Use only Core, actually committed previous Expansion, current planned new entries, whitelisted names and frozen exceptions. Read planned IDs, lemma/POS/sense and morphology hints. Absence from the snapshot means forbidden; do not infer permission from a planned earlier date.

Choose one small, plausible goal and a scene suited to the planned new words. Select review words only when they help that scene. Review 5–8 is a soft target. Omission and defer are preferable to review-only side events. Credit comes from analyzed actual appearances, never the selection list.

Keep Korean very simple and natural. Core repetition and repeated nouns are welcome. No literary synonyms, harder vocabulary, artificial pronoun variation, or filler to stretch short sentences. Draft toward 130–150 eojeol; maintain all hard bounds. Length repair must add a coherent action, necessary exchange or resolution step.

## Short bad-pattern memory

- Avoid `N에게 웃어요` as a mechanically translated reaction. When natural, `N을/를 보고 웃어요` expresses seeing somebody and smiling. Otherwise use a different plausible action or omit the reaction; this is not a forced replacement template.
- Track ownership explicitly: my object, a friend's object, a borrowed object and a gift must not silently become the same ambiguous referent. Repeat the concrete noun and owner when needed. Do not introduce unapproved nominalizations to label ownership.
- Do not use 좋아하다/좋다 as a universal reaction meaning relief, success, amusement or gratitude. Use the intended literal meaning or omit the reaction.
- Avoid literal Japanese-style genitive chaining. Prefer natural noun relations; retain 의 when ownership needs clarification.
- Use simple present for habits. Do not mechanically attach -고 있다 to 자주/매일.
- Do not add unrelated events or unnatural actions to consume review vocabulary.
- Do not expand 말하다 into calling a person, 물어보다 into borrowing, 받다 into holding, or another verb into a missing verb's sense.
- Avoid abrupt subject changes and unclear 이것/그것/여기. Prefer explicit nouns and participants over elegant omission.
- Never add 먼저/같이/조금/아주/정말/천천히 only for sentence length.

After mechanical PASS, independently reread Korean for argument frames, referents, continuity, subject changes, simple readability and review integration. Recheck the Japanese translation against the finalized Korean without silently correcting it. Repair clear problems; do not repair merely to lower semantic warning counts. The author's review is not independent gold or a substitute for human spot review.

For diversity, inspect the last ten scene fingerprints. Change goals, problems, resolutions, participant relationships or actions when a skeleton repeats. Settings may repeat naturally. Genre goals must not force an implausible scene.


## Phase 3.8 sense policy

Use only the Expansion IDs in the current allowed snapshot. Do not substitute a future synonym. 아침 may mean morning or breakfast; 저녁 may mean evening or dinner, under the existing IDs. All other entries receive no inferred sense expansion. Explicit restricted entries are attached to the snapshot with their original notes: use the allowed sense, especially 찍다/photo, 쓰다/write, 들다/hold or lift by hand. Ordinary semantic grammar REVIEW is diagnostic only. Do not repair prose merely to reduce diagnostic counts.
